# PYPY Grid — User Guide Welcome to PYPY Grid, the leading AI-Powered Smart Grid Cybersecurity Research & Simulation SaaS Platform. This guide helps researchers, academics, and security operations personnel navigate the platform, design scenarios, run simulations, and leverage the AI Copilot to analyze power grid resilience. --- ## 1. Introduction & Overview PYPY Grid represents a significant advancement in critical infrastructure cybersecurity, specifically focused on electrical transmission and distribution grids. In modern power systems, the integration of digital SCADA systems, smart meters, and phasor measurement units (PMUs) has introduced a vast cyber-physical attack surface. Securing these architectures requires deep analysis of physical systems coevolving with cyber defense policies. The core philosophy of PYPY Grid is to allow users to safely model, simulate, and study cyberattacks on standard electrical networks without physical risk or disruption. By coupling high-fidelity electrical simulation (using standard Newton-Raphson power flow calculations) with simulated networking layers, users can observe the physical cascading effects of false data injection attacks, coordinated relay tripping, and replay attacks in real time. --- ## 2. Platform Navigation The control center interface is divided into several main sections, accessible via the sidebar menu: 1. **Dashboard**: High-level telemetry visualizations displaying voltage profiles, active/reactive power generation, load distribution, and the master system status. 2. **BCM Center (Business Continuity Management)**: Displays real-time calculations of grid vulnerability, islanding probability, and cutline status. 3. **Research Workspace**: The central hub for defining and executing simulations. Here you choose grid topologies, select scenarios, adjust parameters, and trigger execution sweeps. 4. **Scenario Marketplace**: A catalog of pre-configured threat models and attack profiles mapped directly to the MITRE ATT&CK framework for ICS (Industrial Control Systems). 5. **Reports & Audit**: Access historical verification reports, certification grades, and coevolutionary logs. 6. **AI Copilot**: An interactive chatbot interface backed by a domain-specific LLM trained on smart grid protocols, electrical engineering standards, and cybersecurity frameworks. --- ## 3. Working with Grid Topologies PYPY Grid supports multiple standard IEEE bus configurations, each suitable for different research scales: ### IEEE 14-Bus Test System The IEEE 14-bus test system represents a portion of the American Electric Power System as of February 1962. It consists of: - 14 buses (nodes) - 5 generators - 11 loads (totaling 259 MW and 73.5 MVAR) - 20 transmission lines This topology is recommended for beginners learning the basics of false data injection, state estimation bypass, and simple overcurrent relay tripping. ### IEEE 39-Bus Test System Also known as the 10-generator New England power system. It consists of: - 39 buses - 10 generators - 19 loads - 46 transmission lines This is a standard benchmark topology for studying transient stability, generator outages, and coordinated cyber-physical attack campaigns. The system is divided into multiple subgrids, making it an excellent platform for testing self-healing algorithms and islanding recovery. ### IEEE 57-Bus Test System Consists of 57 buses, 7 generators, 42 loads, and 80 transmission lines. It is suitable for medium-scale simulations analyzing extended betweenness centrality and wide-area monitoring protection systems (WAMPAC). ### IEEE 118-Bus Test System A large-scale representation of a portion of the American Electric Power System (in the Midwestern US). It consists of 118 buses, 54 generators, 99 loads, and 186 transmission lines. This topology presents a high computational scale, ideal for testing machine learning anomaly detection and multi-agent coordination frameworks under cascading failure events. --- ## 4. Setting Up Your First Experiment To launch a simulation: 1. Navigate to the **Research Workspace**. 2. Click **+ Launch New Simulation** to open the wizard. 3. **Step 1: Grid Selection**: Choose your topology (e.g., IEEE 39-bus). 4. **Step 2: Attack Profile**: Choose an attack category from the ICS catalog: - *False Data Injection Attack (FDIA)*: Modifies voltage or phase angle telemetry sent to the SCADA system, attempting to bypass state estimation checks. - *Replay Attack*: Captures normal telemetry and replays it during an active physical manipulation to keep operators blind to critical damage. - *Coordinated Line Trip*: Simultaneously triggers multiple circuit breakers to force cascading line overloads and islanding. 5. **Step 3: Target Nodes**: Select specific buses or transmission lines to target. You can consult the AI Copilot to determine which nodes have the highest structural centrality. 6. **Step 4: Execute**: Click **Run Simulation**. The platform schedules the task on a Celery worker pool and redirects you to the live monitoring dashboard. --- ## 5. Interpreting Simulation Telemetry When a simulation runs, several charts update in real time: - **Voltage Profile (pu)**: Displays the per-unit voltage at each bus. Healthy levels are between 0.95 and 1.05 pu. Values below 0.9 pu indicate severe degradation or imminent brownout. - **Active / Reactive Power**: Charts displaying generator output. Rapid fluctuations suggest generator governors are struggling to balance load. - **Cascading Failures Log**: A step-by-step history showing line overloads, relay trips, and generator shutdowns. - **Resilience Score**: A dynamic percentage representing the health and recovery potential of the grid. It is calculated based on voltage deviation, unserved load ratio, and topological connectivity. --- ## 6. Utilizing the AI Copilot The AI Copilot is more than a chatbot; it is integrated directly into the workspace database. You can ask: - *"Which buses in the IEEE 39-bus grid have the highest vulnerability?"* - *"Help me design an FDIA mitigation script for the IEEE-14 topology."* - *"Summarize the cascading failure sequence of my last experiment."* The Copilot uses Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to inspect your recent experiment records, load the topology parameters, and provide exact physical engineering advice mapped to standard literature. --- ## 7. Advanced Research Techniques Researchers can leverage the API to execute batch sweeps. By scripting against the `/api/experiments` endpoint, you can perform sensitivity analysis on attack magnitudes, evaluate the efficacy of reinforcement learning-based automatic defense agents, and export CSV logs for local analysis in MATLAB or Python (pandapower/PyPSA). For help or support, submit a ticket in the Settings tab or contact research@pypygrid.com.
